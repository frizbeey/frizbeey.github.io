About me

I build mathematical models of how disease moves through populations — and then go into the lab to check whether the parameters are real.

I'm a graduate researcher working at the join between compartmental and spatial epidemic modelling and experimental biology. Most of my work asks the same question in different forms: what does a transmission model need to get right about temperature, vectors and host physiology before its predictions are worth acting on?

Right now I'm splitting my time between simulation work in Python and wet lab training at NIAB, which has been an education in how much of a parameter estimate is buried in the assay that produced it.

Outside research I'm involved in the Jardine Foundation scholar community, and I'm always glad to hear from people working on plant disease, vector-borne transmission, or climate-driven epidemiology.
